GLASSMORPHISM / SOFT GLASS DESIGN LANGUAGE

Use a sophisticated, restrained form of glassmorphism.

IMPORTANT:
Do NOT make the entire website glassmorphic.
Do NOT use glass cards everywhere.
Glass is an accent layer, not the foundation of the UI.

The foundation must remain:
- warm ivory
- clean
- calm
- tactile
- editorial
- spacious

Use "Soft Frosted Glass" selectively for:
- sticky header when scrolling
- floating utility controls
- search overlay
- filter drawer
- mobile bottom navigation where appropriate
- small floating information panels
- selected promotional micro-panels
- elegant modal/dialog surfaces

GLASS MATERIAL CHARACTERISTICS:
- translucent warm-white surface
- subtle opacity
- very soft background blur
- extremely subtle border
- low visual noise
- restrained shadow
- no heavy glow
- no chrome-like reflections
- no excessive transparency
- no exaggerated Apple-style glass everywhere

The glass should feel:
"light, soft, expensive, modern and almost invisible"

It must never feel:
"futuristic, gaming, neon, cyberpunk or over-designed".

When the glass overlays textile photography:
- blur the background enough to preserve readability
- preserve a sense of the textile texture behind the glass
- never allow the textile image to interfere with text readability

Always preserve adequate contrast between foreground and background.
Glass surfaces must never reduce readability merely for visual effect.

Use fallback solid/opaque surfaces when transparency or backdrop blur would harm readability or performance.

TYPOGRAPHY — DISTINCTIVE BUT EXTREMELY COMFORTABLE

Typography is one of the most important visual identifiers of this website.

Use Readex Pro as the primary Arabic-first interface family.

Do not use:
- Cairo
- Tajawal
- Arial
- Tahoma
- generic system Arabic fonts
- overused "modern Arabic startup" typography
- decorative Arabic fonts for UI

The goal is to create a typography identity that feels distinctive without becoming strange or difficult to read.

READex Pro usage:
- Arabic body: 400
- Arabic secondary information: 400
- navigation / labels: 500
- headings: 600
- major display moments: 600
- avoid excessive 700 usage

Do not use heavy bold text everywhere.

LETTERFORM / RHYTHM:
- generous line-height
- comfortable paragraph width
- balanced Arabic word density
- clean separation between labels and content
- avoid tightly packed navigation
- avoid extremely short line-height
- avoid large blocks of dense text

For important editorial headlines, allow more breathing room.
For shopping information, prioritize compact clarity.

LATIN TYPOGRAPHY:
Use the same Readex family where practical for consistency.
Do not mix multiple unrelated typefaces across the UI.

Optional secondary editorial accent:
A very subtle serif may be used only for one or two large campaign/editorial moments if it genuinely improves the brand identity, but never for navigation, product information or checkout.

The overall system should still look cohesive if the serif accent is removed.

TYPOGRAPHIC PERSONALITY:
The website should visually communicate:
fashion
craftsmanship
precision
calm confidence
modernity

It should NOT communicate:
technology startup
gaming
generic marketplace
corporate banking
cheap fast-fashion template

TYPOGRAPHY TEST:
Every major screen must look beautiful in Arabic before considering the design complete.
Do not create a beautiful Latin interface and simply replace the text with Arabic.

RTL TYPOGRAPHIC BALANCE:
Pay special attention to:
- Arabic punctuation
- Arabic/Latin mixing
- numerals
- prices
- dates
- measurements
- product specifications
- phone numbers
- navigation alignment

The Arabic rhythm must feel native and intentional.

VISUAL COMFORT SYSTEM

The entire visual system should be designed to reduce visual fatigue.

Use:
- large whitespace blocks
- predictable spacing
- restrained borders
- limited accent colors
- soft neutrals
- consistent alignment
- clear grouping
- strong hierarchy
- moderate image density
- controlled card density

Avoid:
- excessive borders
- excessive shadows
- excessive gradients
- visual vibration
- too many competing focal points
- too many badges
- too many CTAs
- unnecessary animations

Create "quiet zones" between major sections.

The user should feel:
"I can browse here for a long time without getting tired."

The design should be visually interesting because of:
- textile photography
- composition
- typography
- subtle materiality
- spacing
- editorial rhythm

Not because of dozens of visual effects.

ETHICAL CONVERSION DESIGN

The commercial design should maximize clarity, confidence and purchase readiness.

Do NOT optimize conversion through manipulation.

Instead use:
- clear value communication
- friction reduction
- information hierarchy
- product confidence
- authentic social proof
- transparent pricing
- clear shipping information
- clear return/exchange information
- clear product specifications
- relevant recommendations
- meaningful calls to action

Psychological principles to apply:
- cognitive fluency
- progressive disclosure
- choice architecture
- social proof
- authority through expertise
- uncertainty reduction
- commitment/progress
- perceived control
- loss aversion only when factually justified

NEVER use:
- fake countdowns
- fake scarcity
- fake reviews
- fake stock numbers
- fake discounts
- hidden fees
- forced subscriptions
- forced account creation
- deceptive default selections
- misleading button hierarchy
- confusing cancellation
- disguised advertising

The goal is:
"make the correct decision easier"
not:
"pressure the user into purchasing".

DEMO / CLIENT PRESENTATION MODE

Do not use real products, real inventory or fabricated business claims.

Create the interface as a polished high-fidelity demonstration.

Where products are normally required:
- use elegant neutral textile placeholders
OR
- use clearly labeled "Demo / Sample" content
OR
- use abstract fabric texture placeholders that demonstrate layout without pretending to represent real inventory

Do not fabricate:
- fake customer reviews
- fake sales numbers
- fake stock levels
- fake ratings
- fake order history
- fake shipping promises
- fake prices presented as real business information

The prototype should demonstrate the SYSTEM, not pretend that the store is already operational.
